<section class="hub-section hub-section-sand hub-full-bleed" aria-labelledby="patterns-heading">
<div class="container"><div class="hub-heading"><p class="home-kicker">Work pattern</p><h2 id="patterns-heading">How the work changes</h2><p>The first choice is the relationship between the software and the work you do today. Each pattern can use deterministic code, an AI model, an agent, or a combination.</p></div>
<div class="row hub-action-grid">
<div class="col-md-4"><article class="panel panel-default hub-action-card"><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-repeat" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Automate</h3><p>Keep the job and output the same while software handles repeatable steps. Start here when you already know what correct looks like and can return to the manual process if needed.</p><p><strong>Example:</strong> Turn a recurring spreadsheet cleanup into an inspectable pipeline that previews changes before export.</p></article></div>
<div class="col-md-4"><article class="panel panel-default hub-action-card"><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-stats" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Upgrade</h3><p>Keep the purpose of the work while giving people a more useful result. The new output might be interactive, current, searchable, or available without another meeting.</p><p><strong>Example:</strong> Replace a recurring status deck with an accessible page that shows when its source data was refreshed.</p></article></div>
<div class="col-md-4"><article class="panel panel-default hub-action-card"><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-flash" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Invent</h3><p>Create a bounded capability that was not feasible as a manual job. Treat adoption and usefulness as questions to test because there is no proven process to copy.</p><p><strong>Example:</strong> Monitor approved public sources for specific changes and route relevant findings to a person for review.</p></article></div>
</div></div>
</section>

<section class="hub-section hub-section-dark hub-full-bleed" aria-labelledby="delivery-heading">
<div class="container"><div class="hub-heading"><p class="home-kicker">Delivery class</p><h2 id="delivery-heading">Match the controls to who depends on it</h2><p>Durability, access, support, and testing increase as a build moves beyond its original creator. A temporary build still follows UC San Diego requirements for data, security, privacy, and appropriate use.</p></div>
<div class="row hub-number-grid">
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-3"><article><span>01</span><h3>Prototype</h3><p>Answers a question or tests an interaction with approved, representative material. Keep it isolated, record what it proves, and stop or revise it when the test ends.</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-3"><article><span>02</span><h3>Personal or team workflow</h3><p>Handles a recurring need for a known, bounded group. It needs reliable results, an approved workspace, a review step, and a way to recover when it fails.</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-3"><article><span>03</span><h3>Supported service</h3><p>Serves people beyond the builder's team. Add a named owner, accessibility, privacy and security review, realistic-load testing, support, monitoring, and change management.</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-3"><article><span>04</span><h3>Public product</h3><p>Serves an open or general audience. This requires a formal product and service process with ongoing ownership. It is rarely the right first citizen-development project.</p></article></div>
</div>
<aside class="hub-note-card citizen-note-card"><strong>Time-bounded software needs an exit plan</strong><span>A prototype, team workflow, or supported service may exist for one event or period. Name the end date, owner, data-retention plan, and retirement steps when you start.</span></aside>
</div>
</section>

<section class="hub-section" aria-labelledby="opportunities-heading">
<div class="hub-heading"><p class="home-kicker">Opportunity scan</p><h2 id="opportunities-heading">Where to look in your day-to-day work</h2><p>Look for work you repeat, explain, reconcile, or review. The examples below can start a conversation. Each still needs the appropriate review before work begins.</p></div>
<div class="row hub-action-grid">
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-4"><article class="panel panel-default hub-action-card"><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-blackboard" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Presentation work</h3><p>Turn a repeated explanation into an accessible walkthrough, comparison, calculator, status page, or interactive explainer.</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-4"><article class="panel panel-default hub-action-card"><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-pencil" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Content work</h3><p>Build a reviewed pipeline that turns approved source material into a draft summary, update, brief, or set of channel-specific versions.</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-4"><article class="panel panel-default hub-action-card"><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-signal" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Data work</h3><p>Reconcile exports, repeat an analysis, surface exceptions, or replace a recurring snapshot with a source-backed dashboard.</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-4"><article class="panel panel-default hub-action-card"><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-file" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Document work</h3><p>Extract approved fields, fill a template, convert files, or link a finding back to the passage a reviewer needs to inspect.</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-4"><article class="panel panel-default hub-action-card"><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-inbox" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Inbox and intake work</h3><p>Organize submissions, identify missing information, route a request, or prepare an unsent response for a person to check.</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-4"><article class="panel panel-default hub-action-card"><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-list-alt" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Administrative work</h3><p>Assemble a briefing, monitor a deadline, update a checklist, or coordinate repeatable steps across approved systems.</p></article></div>
</div>
</section>

<section class="hub-section" aria-labelledby="first-project-heading">
<div class="hub-heading"><p class="home-kicker">First project</p><h2 id="first-project-heading">Choose something bounded and reviewable</h2><p>A useful starter project has a known result, limited access, and a clear fallback. Write down the answers below before opening a builder or asking an agent to act.</p></div>
<div class="row hub-principle-grid">
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-4"><article><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-repeat" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Recurring task</h3><p>What do you do often enough that improving it would matter?</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-4"><article><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-ok-circle" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Known result</h3><p>What does a correct output look like, and what source can you check it against?</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-4"><article><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-lock" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Approved boundary</h3><p>Which people, systems, and data are in scope? Which are excluded?</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-4"><article><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-eye-open" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Human review</h3><p>Who inspects the result before it changes a record, reaches another person, or informs a decision?</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-4"><article><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-backward" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Manual fallback</h3><p>Can you stop the workflow and return to the current process without losing access or data?</p></article></div>
<div class="col-sm-6 col-md-4"><article><span class="glyphicon glyphicon-user" aria-hidden="true"></span><h3>Named owner</h3><p>Who decides whether to keep, change, support, or retire the build?</p></article></div>
</div>
<aside class="hub-note-card"><strong>Check what already exists</strong><span>Use an approved campus service when it meets the need. Build the missing workflow, connection, or experience when the benefit justifies owning it.</span></aside>
</section>
