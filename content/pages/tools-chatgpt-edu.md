<h2 id="chatgpt-edu-intro-heading">A systemwide agreement with OpenAI</h2>
<p class="hub-lede">On November 19, the University of California announced a systemwide agreement with OpenAI for ChatGPT Edu (<a href="https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/press-room/uc-expands-collaboration-openai-offering-free-access-students-faculty-and-staff-secure">read the UC Office of the President announcement</a>). Many of you have seen the news and are asking what it means here.</p>
<p>As part of the UC system, UC San Diego will make ChatGPT Edu available under that agreement. It is <strong>not available here yet</strong>. <strong>Each campus is responsible for its own rollout</strong>, and we are working on ours now.</p>
</div>
</div>
</section>

<h2 id="chatgpt-edu-announcement-heading">What the announcement says</h2>
<p>The announcement describes an expanded systemwide agreement that covers OpenAI’s tools, including ChatGPT and Codex. It says the data of people who choose to use them will be protected. Under the terms UC negotiated, that means:</p>
<ul>
<li>OpenAI does not use what you enter, or the responses you receive, to train or improve its models.</li>
<li>The university, not OpenAI, owns that content.</li>
<li>UC’s security terms apply and take priority over OpenAI’s standard terms. They include a requirement that OpenAI report a breach to UC.</li>
</ul>
<p>These protections apply to accounts in the university’s workspace. They do not apply to personal ChatGPT accounts, and they do not mean every kind of university data may be entered. Guidance on which data is appropriate will come with the rollout.</p>
<p>The announcement also says UC does not require these tools, and that faculty decide whether and how AI is used in their courses.</p>
<p>That describes what the agreement makes possible across the UC system. It does not set a date for any campus, and it does not mean every tool it names, Codex included, will be turned on here at the same time or in the same way. Which tools are offered at UC San Diego, to whom, and in what order will be set by the campus operating model, our plan for how the service will run here.</p>
<p><strong>We are not publishing dates yet.</strong> We will share timing when we can stand behind it.</p>

<h2 id="chatgpt-edu-why-heading">Why it is not available on day one</h2>
<p>A systemwide agreement sets the terms the university has with OpenAI. It does not decide how the service runs on each campus. That part is ours to work out: who gets access to what, how people request it, and how it is supported over time.</p>
<p>The answers depend on the details of the contract. What it provides is limited, and the current arrangement <strong>covers one year</strong>. Whatever we set up has to work for a campus of our size, distribute access fairly across students, faculty and staff, and still make sense when that year ends.</p>
<p>The operating model is still being developed. <strong>The Academic Senate will be consulted</strong> on it, and it will be shared through the appropriate campus channels before it takes effect.</p>

<h2 id="chatgpt-edu-credits-heading">What an account includes and what uses credits</h2>
<p>As OpenAI describes it, ChatGPT Edu has two kinds of use.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Everyday use that comes with an account.</strong> OpenAI’s fast general-purpose models for chat, writing, search and working with files, plus a small daily allowance of features such as image generation and deep research.</li>
<li><strong>Advanced use that draws on credits.</strong> More capable reasoning models, longer research and agent tasks, and coding tools such as Codex. Each use subtracts credits, and heavier tasks subtract more.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Credits are limited.</strong> Within the terms of the one-year contract, the campus has a set allocation that is bought in advance and shared by everyone, and OpenAI sets how many credits each feature uses. If use goes over that allocation during the year, <strong>the campus is charged for the difference</strong>. For that reason, access to the features that use credits has to be planned: who can use them, how much, and what happens when a group needs more than its share.</p>
<p>UC San Diego has not yet decided which of these features will be turned on, or how credits will be shared. Until it does, descriptions from OpenAI or from other UC campuses may not match what an account here will include.</p>
