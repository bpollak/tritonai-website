<h2 id="chatgpt-edu-intro-heading">A systemwide agreement with OpenAI</h2>
<p class="hub-lede">On November 19, the University of California announced a systemwide agreement with OpenAI for ChatGPT Edu (<a href="https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/press-room/uc-expands-collaboration-openai-offering-free-access-students-faculty-and-staff-secure">read the UC Office of the President announcement</a>). Many of you have seen the news and are asking what it means here.</p>
<p>As part of the UC system, UC San Diego will make ChatGPT Edu available under that agreement. It is not available here yet. Each campus is responsible for its own rollout, and we are working on ours now.</p>
</div>
</div>
</section>

<h2 id="chatgpt-edu-announcement-heading">What the announcement says</h2>
<p>The announcement describes an expanded systemwide agreement that covers OpenAI’s tools, including ChatGPT and Codex, at no cost to individuals and with guardrails. It also says UC does not require these tools, and that faculty decide whether and how AI is used in their courses.</p>
<p>That describes what the agreement makes possible across the UC system. It does not set a date for any campus, and it does not mean every tool it names, Codex included, will be turned on here at the same time or in the same way. Which tools are offered at UC San Diego, to whom, and in what order will be set by our operating model.</p>
<p>We are not publishing dates yet. We will share timing when we can stand behind it.</p>
</div>
</div>
</section>

<h2 id="chatgpt-edu-why-heading">Why it is not available on day one</h2>
<p>A systemwide agreement sets the terms the university has with OpenAI. It does not decide how the service runs on each campus. That part is ours to work out: who gets access to what, how people request it, and how it is supported over time.</p>
<p>The answers depend on the details of the contract. What it provides is not unlimited, and the current arrangement covers one year. Whatever we set up has to distribute access fairly across students, faculty and staff, work for a campus of our size, and still make sense when that year ends.</p>
<p>An operating model is being developed and will be socialized appropriately through appropriate channels before it’s enacted.</p>

<h2 id="chatgpt-edu-credits-heading">What an account includes and what uses credits</h2>
<p>As OpenAI describes it, ChatGPT Edu has two layers.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Everyday use that comes with an account.</strong> OpenAI’s fast general-purpose models for chat, writing, search and working with files, plus small daily amounts of features such as image generation and deep research.</li>
<li><strong>Advanced use that draws on credits.</strong> More capable reasoning models, longer research and agent tasks, and coding tools such as Codex. Each use subtracts credits, and heavier tasks subtract more.</li>
</ul>
<p>Credits are limited. Within the terms of the one-year contract, the campus has a set allocation that is bought in advance and shared by everyone, and OpenAI sets how many credits each feature uses. If use goes over that allocation during the year, the campus is charged for the difference. Access to the credit-based features has to be planned for that reason: who can use them, how much, and what happens when a group needs more than its share.</p>
<p>How these layers will be set up at UC San Diego has not been decided. What an account includes here may differ from what OpenAI or other campuses describe.</p>
