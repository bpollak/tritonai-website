## What's New in Your AI Tools

### Google Gemini & Workspace

-   **[Gmail Live is rolling out to paid Workspace users](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/gmail-live-is-rolling-out-to-paid-Workspace-users.html)** — Announced October 15, gradual rollout (up to 15 days for feature visibility): Gmail Live brings Gemini-powered voice search to the Gmail mobile apps on iOS and Android. Tap the Live entry point in the Gmail search bar, ask a question naturally ("When is my next mandatory training due?" or "Show me the latest update from my manager"), and hear a synthesized answer drawn from your inbox. An email source card now appears at every conversational turn so you can see exactly which message the answer came from, and you can pick your preferred voice. The feature is ON by default for organizations with Gemini in Workspace access and respects existing smart features and personalization admin settings. One caveat for now: it's currently US-only with EN-US as the language, so most campus users won't see it immediately, but it's a clear signal of where Google is taking search across Workspace.

-   **[Gmail's AI Inbox is rolling out to paid Workspace users](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/gmails-ai-inbox-is-rolling-out-to-paid-Workspace-users.html)** — Also announced October 15: AI Inbox, first launched earlier this year, is expanding from its initial audience to paid Workspace editions. It automatically extracts suggested to-dos (action items, deliverables, and deadlines) from your email and summarizes priority work updates, so the important items surface instead of sinking. Rapid Release domains started seeing it October 12; Scheduled Release domains start October 26. Google notes AI Inbox will roll out later to Starter and Education editions in the coming months, so campus Gmail users should watch for it rather than expect it this week.

-   **[Skills arrive in the Gemini app; the Workspace launch slips](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/an-update-on-launch-of-skills-in-gemini-app-and-workspace.html)** — Announced October 13: skills — reusable, stackable custom instructions that replace Gems — started rolling out in the Gemini app for all users, with completion expected by mid-November. Skill sharing and a Google Drive integration follow in the coming weeks. The bigger news for admins: the launch of skills in Workspace (Workspace Studio and Workspace apps) is delayed until further notice; Google had previously targeted Rapid Release completion by October 12 and a Scheduled Release start of October 19, and now says it will confirm a new timeline. The Gems retirement clock is still running: Gems move to Settings in the Gemini app on November 17, business and enterprise users lose Gems no sooner than March 1, 2027, and education users no sooner than June 1, 2027, with remaining Gems auto-migrated to draft skills. There are no admin controls for skills in the Gemini app; Workspace skills do have admin controls and are limited to users 18+. If your team built Gems for recurring workflows, start planning the manual recreation now — skills don't sync between the Gemini app and Workspace.

### Copilot for Microsoft 365

-   **[Office is now built into the new Copilot app](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoft-copilot-blog/introducing-office-in-the-new-microsoft-copilot-app/4561230)** — Announced October 9: you can now create, edit, and collaborate on Word, Excel, and PowerPoint files directly inside the new Microsoft Copilot app, without hopping out to the individual Office apps. Combined with Copilot's chat and agents, the Copilot app becomes a single surface where you can ask about your work, act on it, and produce the document, spreadsheet, or deck — all in one place, inside Microsoft 365's existing security and compliance boundary.

-   **[Copilot Notebooks gains delegated work, Office-grade editing, and more reference types](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoft-copilot-blog/whats-new-in-notebooks--october-2026/4559105)** — Also in the October 9 roundup: Copilot Notebooks (the AI workspace that gathers your references and project context in one place) now lets you delegate simple work to Copilot, edit Word, Excel, and PowerPoint files with the full power of the Office apps, and build interactive reports — plus Markdown, TXT, and RTF files can be added as references alongside existing source types. If your team is evaluating NotebookLM-style grounded workspaces, Notebooks is Microsoft's answer and it's moving fast.

-   **[Copilot for legal is generally available — 20+ purpose-built legal skills](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoft-copilot-blog/introducing-microsoft-copilot-for-legal/4564246)** — Announced October 15: Microsoft shipped more than 20 legal-specific skills into Copilot, plus partner plugins from 25 legal-technology vendors (iManage, NetDocuments, Docusign, Adobe Acrobat Sign, Everlaw, Relativity, Harvey, and others) that are beginning to roll out. In Word, Copilot drafts new agreements from approved precedents and reviews contracts against your playbook, proposing changes as native tracked changes that apply only with your approval. In Cowork, it handles multi-step work: building obligation trackers across contracts, drafting the transmittal email, and adding deadlines to your calendar. The Word plugin is enabled by default for commercial Microsoft 365 Copilot subscribers (governed by the "Legal Skills in M365 Copilot in Word" policy); in Cowork, users add the plugin themselves and admins govern it on the Agent Tools page. Directly relevant to campus offices that live in contract review — and a preview of how vertical skill packs are coming to Copilot generally.

-   **Admin note: Copilot offer structure changes December 1** — Per Microsoft's October partner announcements (updated October 14): beginning December 1, 2026, new Microsoft 365 Copilot and Copilot Business offers can be purchased without a qualifying Microsoft 365 base subscription, and those new offers replace the add-on offers for new purchases. Separately, usage-based billing will be default-on for new Copilot Business licenses starting December 1, with default spending limits. This extends last week's billing-split item: the license line item keeps shrinking as a share of the real cost story, so units budgeting Copilot renewals should model credits and spending policies now, not in December.

## TritonAI News

-   **[UC San Diego awarded $2.35 million from Google to advance AI-driven climate modeling](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/uc-san-diego-awarded-2.35-million-from-google-to-advance-ai-driven-climate-modeling)** — Announced October 14: Duncan Watson-Parris, assistant professor jointly appointed at the Halıcıoğlu School of Data Science and Scripps Institution of Oceanography, received $2.35 million through the Google.org Impact Challenge: AI for Science, a $36 million global initiative. The project uses AI agents both to improve how existing ocean data is represented in climate models and to identify where new observations would add the most value — including measurements from specially adapted Argo floats that capture small-scale ocean mixing, a process current models handle poorly. The work builds on the campus GAIA Initiative, which brings together researchers using AI to understand Earth's natural systems, and it's a strong example of the agentic-AI approach applied to hard scientific problems rather than office workflows.

-   **[The 2027 EDUCAUSE Top 10 puts "determining where AI adds real value" at No. 1](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/05/higher-ed-tech-officers-navigating-age)** — Unveiled at EDUCAUSE 2026 in Denver and covered by Inside Higher Ed on October 5: for the first time, an AI-related item tops EDUCAUSE's annual list, which draws on a survey of 882 technology leaders across hundreds of institutions. The framing is notable — not "adopt more AI" but figure out where it demonstrably serves the mission, alongside future-proofing students, responsible innovation structures, and a strong data-governance backbone. EDUCAUSE's Mark McCormack called it urgency rather than consensus: adopting technology because it's there "isn't good enough." That value-first posture is the same discipline behind TritonAI's task-specific assistants — tools that meet people where they already work — and it's a useful citation when a unit asks why we build vertical assistants instead of pointing everyone at one generic chatbot.

-   **[Peer institutions are formalizing faculty-led AI governance too](https://www.colorado.edu/today/2026/10/08/faculty-assembly-introduces-ai-resolution-hears-housing-assistance-update-more)** — October 8 at CU Boulder: the faculty assembly introduced an AI resolution, moving shared governance formally into how the campus sets AI policy. It's the same direction UC San Diego took with its departmental AI deliberation — asking faculty bodies to work through AI's implications discipline by discipline before the university writes its next set of principles and policies. As more campuses land on deliberation-first models, the approach stops looking like an outlier and starts looking like the emerging standard for getting AI governance right.
