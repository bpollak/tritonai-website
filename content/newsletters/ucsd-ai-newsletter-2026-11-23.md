## What's New in Your AI Tools

### Google Gemini & Workspace

-   **[Gemini Notebook can now turn your sources into an interactive site](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/transform-your-notes-and-research-into-dynamic-tools-with-Gemini-Notebook.html)** — Announced November 18, rolling out gradually from November 16 (up to 15 days). Ask for it in plain language — "make me a site" plus a description of what you want — and Gemini Notebook builds a working page out of the sources already in the notebook, then gives you a link to open in a new tab or share. Google's own examples are a study aid (an interactive periodic table, a historical timeline), a data dashboard, and a project tracker; the realistic campus version is a policy brief, an onboarding guide, or a set of accreditation evidence that a colleague can click through instead of reading thirty pages. There's no admin control specific to sites — admins still decide whether Gemini Notebook is on for the organization. Two cautions worth stating plainly: the site is generated from your sources, so check the numbers and claims before anyone relies on it, and a shared site is a publication, not a scratch pad — treat the sharing decision with the same care as the notebook itself. Web only for now, 18 and older; mobile is coming.

-   **[Skills in the Gemini app can now be shared with colleagues, and can pull in files from Drive, Gemini Notebook, and Photos](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/share-skills-add-files-from-Drive-Notebook-Photos-to-skills-Gemini-app.html)** — Announced November 16, rollout began November 12. This is the follow-up to the Workspace skills launch we covered two weeks ago, and it closes the obvious gap: a skill can now leave your account. Share any skill from the Skills page with View or Edit permission; recipients are notified by email, review it before installing, and then Gemini applies it automatically when relevant or they @mention it. Skills can also reference Drive files, notebooks, and Photos alongside uploads — and Drive files and notebooks stay live, so a skill grounded in your unit's style guide always works from the current version. The limits: sharing and access management work only in the Gemini app on the web, not mobile; if a skill includes Drive files you have to share those files too; uploads cap at 100 MB per skill. The governance detail matters more than the feature — skills follow your organization's Drive sharing settings, so if Drive files can be shared outside the organization, skills can leave with them. Skill sharing is on by default and can be turned off in the Admin console.

-   **[Gemini-powered data classification in Google Drive is generally available](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/gemini-powered-data-classification-in-Google-Drive-is-now-generally-available.html)** — Announced November 17. Until now, AI classification meant hand-labeling training files so a model could learn what each classification level looks like. Admins can now write plain-language instructions instead, test them in the Admin console against real files to preview which label Gemini would apply, and watch near-real-time reporting on how many files were labeled and how often users accepted the label. That matters because labels are what drive data loss prevention policies, retention rules, and audit investigations — and, in Google's framing, what keeps agentic workflows from reaching and acting on sensitive data they shouldn't see. The limits are real: one active Gemini instruction model per organization and five classification models in total (custom-trained plus Gemini); files need roughly 50 words of readable text; client-side encrypted files are skipped; Gemini won't overwrite a label a person or a DLP rule applied; and the first backfill scan starts within one to two weeks but can take weeks or months to finish. This is admin-side work — if your unit keeps sensitive data in Shared Drives, raise it with IT and security rather than waiting for a user-facing toggle.

### Copilot for Microsoft 365

-   **[Office now runs inside the Copilot app, and Copilot Home is generally available — the Ignite announcements that actually shipped](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoft-copilot-blog/office-at-ignite-2026-from-apps-to-agentic-system-of-work/4572226)** — Announced November 17 at Ignite. Office in the Copilot app is generally available: ask Copilot for a launch brief or a budget model and Word or Excel opens beside the conversation, where you can shape it with prompts or click in and type, bring teammates in for live coauthoring, and hand off to the full desktop apps when you need them. Every file saves to OneDrive and inherits the permissions, sensitivity labels, and compliance controls your organization already applies, and document creation is included in a Copilot subscription rather than metered separately. Copilot Home, which puts Chat and Cowork in one place and suggests handing a task from one to the other, also shipped. Be careful with the rest of the keynote: Today (a morning brief across mail, calendar, chats, and meetings, with drafts and scheduling proposals prepared for your approval), Pages, and Workspaces (the successor to Copilot Notebooks, where a team and its agents share one space) are Frontier early-access features, not general availability, and Autopilot is only beginning to roll out there. Two practical notes: Office in Copilot needs an eligible paid Copilot license — a standard Microsoft 365 license doesn't include it — so confirm licensing with IT before planning around it, and don't commit a workflow to Today or Workspaces until they leave Frontier.

### Zoom

-   **[Zoom Phone's AI call summaries can now redact sensitive data and trigger escalation workflows](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0085380)** — In the November 22 release. Admins can apply your organization's personal and sensitive data groups to AI call summaries and transcripts, so redacted values appear as data-group identifiers in call history instead of the actual content — and the same redaction covers recordings, voicemail, videomail, SMS, and MMS ([redaction details](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0074867)). The more interesting piece is for anyone running a queue: an agentic workflow can now trigger when a voicemail goes unheard past a threshold you set, check the message's live status, and escalate — forward it, post to a Zoom chat, send an SMS or email, or open a ticket in a downstream system — while suppressing the escalation entirely if someone listened to or deleted it during the wait. Admins can also restrict who can read AI call summaries to trusted IP ranges. All of it is account-level configuration that depends on the Zoom AI features and credits your unit has, so for a service desk or a shared department line, these are the two settings to ask IT about.

## TritonAI News

### ChatGPT Edu at UC San Diego

-   **[ChatGPT Edu is coming to UC San Diego under the new UC agreement — but it isn't available yet, and there's nothing to sign up for](https://tritonai.ucsd.edu/tools/chatgpt-edu.html)** — On November 19, the UC Office of the President [announced an expanded systemwide agreement with OpenAI](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/press-room/uc-expands-collaboration-openai-offering-free-access-students-faculty-and-staff-secure) covering ChatGPT and Codex, at no cost to individuals, with contractual data protections and AI literacy training through OpenAI Academy. UC San Diego will offer ChatGPT Edu under that agreement, but each campus runs its own rollout: implementation planning is underway here, no launch dates are published yet, and you don't need to sign up, submit a request, or change anything now. The campus page makes two things clear that are easy to get wrong from the systemwide announcement. First, the agreement covers one year with a limited, pre-purchased credit allocation shared across campus, and if use exceeds it the university is charged — everyday chat, writing, search, and file work sit inside an account, while advanced reasoning models, long research and agent runs, and coding tools like Codex draw on credits, and UC San Diego hasn't decided which credit-based features it will enable or how credits will be shared. Second, the operating model goes to the Academic Senate for consultation and will be shared through campus channels before it takes effect. Two things to do in the meantime: Rady already runs ChatGPT Edu under its own agreement and Rady users can keep working as they do today, and if your department is about to buy or renew ChatGPT subscriptions, contact [tritonai@ucsd.edu](mailto:tritonai@ucsd.edu) first, because the systemwide deal may change what you should be buying.

### TritonAI Harness Chat for iPhone

-   **[Harness Chat brings your work computer to your phone — pilot support page and privacy statement published](https://tritonai.ucsd.edu/developer-apis/harness-phone-app.html)** — Published November 22. The iPhone companion to the TritonAI Harness works the way it sounds: you ask in your own words on the phone, the assistant on your Mac or PC does the work with its files, tools, and connected systems, and the answer comes back in one conversation. It needs the Harness installed and open on your computer and iOS 18 or later; on the same Wi-Fi it works as is, and anywhere else both devices also need Tailscale. Setup takes about two minutes: on the computer, Settings, Sharing & access, turn on Network access, choose Create link, then Share to show a square code that works once for about five minutes; in the app, scan it or paste the link. Expect one conversation with no projects, threads, or model choices, history of everything you've asked, approvals before it opens a web page (you can set it to ask before every step), and email drafts written for you to review and send yourself. The failure modes are documented too: an expired link needs a fresh one, and if your computer is asleep a message waits on the phone and sends when it reconnects. The app is in pilot, and the TritonAI team shares the install link with pilot participants — email [tritonai@ucsd.edu](mailto:tritonai@ucsd.edu) to ask about joining.

### TritonAI Gateway

-   **[KnowLine is now available through the TritonAI LLM Gateway](https://tritonai.ucsd.edu/about/tritonai-updates.html)** — Posted November 17. KnowLine-35B-A3B-Gen1.5 is a decision model rather than a chat model: instead of prose it returns probabilities — for a yes/no question, across a set of category choices, or against a rubric's scores. That makes it a classifier you can put in front of a workflow, so a request gets routed ("facilities or IT?", "does this submission meet the rubric?") with a confidence number attached and the uncertain cases still land with a person. Read the [publisher's model card](https://huggingface.co/PelaAI/KnowLine-35B-A3B-Gen1.5) for capabilities and limits, and the [Gateway model details](https://docs.tritonai.ucsd.edu/llm-api/models) for current routes and limits.

### Around Campus

-   **[RAIN has grown into a campuswide student AI network — and it is now the intake path for student Gateway access](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/uc-san-diego-students-build-campuswide-network-to-accelerate-ai-innovation)** — Published November 19. The Real-world Artificial Intelligence Network, founded during the 2025–26 academic year, now coordinates upward of 7,000 students, more than 50 faculty members, and 40-plus student organizations plus every start-up accelerator and entrepreneurship group on campus into one ecosystem, advised by faculty from the Halıcıoğlu School of Data Science and Computing and the School of Biological Sciences, and backed by alumni and industry partners. Chancellor Khosla's framing is that what makes it work is being student-driven. The piece that matters for staff and faculty: as of November 19, student projects request campus-hosted model access through RAIN's Raindrop portal instead of the staff and faculty Gateway intake — RAIN reviews the project and issues the Gateway key on approval, with the steps on the [student access page](https://tritonai.ucsd.edu/developer-apis/start.html#student-access). For faculty, that's the referral when a student asks how to get model access for a project; for staff, it's a partner for applied AI work that involves students.
