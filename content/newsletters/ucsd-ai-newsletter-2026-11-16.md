## What's New in Your AI Tools

### Google Gemini & Workspace

-   **[Skills in Google Workspace and Workspace Studio are rolling out — instructions your team writes once and reuses](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/skills-in-workspace.html)** — Announced November 10. A skill is a saved set of instructions — your unit's rules, templates, and reference files — that Gemini applies when it's relevant, so nobody re-types the same context every week. You can build skills from scratch or from templates in Workspace Studio, edit the underlying SKILL.md file directly, test the output before turning a skill on, and collaborate on one in a Google Doc. They also work in context: prompt Gemini inside Gmail, Docs, Slides, Drive, or Chat to create and refine a skill without leaving the app. Once enabled, Gemini can apply a skill automatically or you can call it explicitly with an @ mention — including several at once — and skills can kick off Workspace Studio flows. Two limits worth knowing: skills aren't available in Sheets or Meet yet and support English only, and a shared skill arrives as an independent copy, so your later edits won't reach people who already saved it. For units that answer the same kinds of requests all week, this is the closest thing Workspace has to the skills people are already recording in the TritonAI Harness.

-   **[Gemini in Gmail is now available to every Workspace user 18 and older](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/expanding-gemini-in-gmail-to-more-Google-Workspace-users.html)** — Announced November 10, rolling out gradually over 15 days. Two features previously limited to certain editions and consumer accounts are now standard for work and school accounts: **AI Overview** summaries at the top of long threads, and **Help me write** for drafting or refining a message from a short prompt. Both are on by default for eligible users and can be turned off individually, and admins can disable Gemini features in Gmail at the domain or organizational-unit level. If the inbox has been the one place Gemini never showed up for your team, check again this week.

-   **[Workspace Studio flows can now pause and resume on a schedule](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/11/pause-and-schedule-automated-workflows-with-the-new-Wait-until-step-in-Workspace-Studio.html)** — Rolling out from November 9. A new **Wait until** step pauses a flow at any point — including inside a conditional branch or a loop — and resumes it at a set date and time, or after a duration of up to 14 days, with the Activity tab logging when it's scheduled to continue. Before this, any workflow with a delay (a follow-up email three days after a meeting, staggered onboarding reminders, a Monday-morning digest) had to be split across separate scheduled flows. Now it's one flow, no code, and there's no admin setting involved. Available in Workspace Studio for Education Fundamentals, Standard, and Plus.

### Copilot for Microsoft 365

-   **[Copilot connectors can now change things, not just read them](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoft-copilot-blog/turn-copilot-insights-into-action-with-connector-write-capabilities/4571160)** — Announced November 9: connector write actions are generally available in Copilot Chat and Cowork, so Copilot can create, update, or delete information in connected applications — turning a meeting transcript into a CRM activity, or a pile of Outlook escalations into labelled, summarized issues — without leaving the conversation. Actions run on the user's own permissions in the connected system, and new write tools start at **Needs approval**: you see the proposed action and choose Allow once, Allow for conversation, or Always allow. Write actions are coming to Copilot in the Office apps next. "Always allow" is the choice to think about before clicking, since it removes your review step for that tool from then on.

-   **[Plugins move into Copilot's navigation, and a public Plugin Catalog opens November 17](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoft-copilot-blog/plugins-at-microsoft-ignite-2026/4569951)** — Posted November 12 ahead of Ignite. With the new Copilot Home bringing Chat and Cowork together, plugins — the umbrella over skills, tools and connectors, and agents — begin appearing in the app's navigation, so familiar agents stay easy to find while the wider catalog becomes discoverable. Microsoft says admins need no back-end changes; the existing plugin registry still governs publishing, approval, and discovery. The public catalog for browsing the broader plugin ecosystem launches November 17.

### Zoom AI

-   **[Zoom has retired the "AI Companion" name — the features are unchanged, the label isn't](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0080363)** — In the November 16 release, Zoom dropped the AI Companion brand. Meeting summaries, chat compose, scheduling assistance, smart recording and the rest remain available throughout Zoom Workplace, but the AI Companion settings page is now just **AI**, and the help documentation is being renamed to match. The same release adds a **participant list inside each meeting summary** — everyone who joined at any point, reachable from the Zoom Hub, web portal, or calendar, and included when the summary is exported as a PDF (email recipients see up to 20 names plus a link to the full list). Practical upshot: if you've been telling people to "turn on AI Companion," the setting they need is now called AI.

-   **[Scheduling delegates can manage the meeting summaries they arrange for you](https://support.zoom.us/hc/en-us/articles/204758419)** — Generally available as of November 9: users can let a delegate manage AI meeting summaries for everything on their calendar, for meetings the delegate scheduled, or for any meeting scheduled by any delegate — viewing, editing, and sharing summaries on the delegator's behalf. It's opt-in, and it's the piece that was missing for executive assistants and anyone whose calendar is run by someone else.

## TritonAI News

### TritonAI Harness

-   **[Harness v0.4.2 is out — a steadying release that makes project work easier to navigate and fixes the integrations people lean on](https://tritonai.ucsd.edu/developer-apis/harness-release-notes.html)** — Published November 14, v0.4.2 follows the project-and-skills overhaul in v0.4.1 about three weeks ago, and this one is refinement rather than new concepts. **Project Home** is clearer and settings are reorganized so project activity and next steps are easier to find, and conversation search now finds work by title, branch, pull request, project, and message content — which matters once you have a few months of projects behind you. Browser access is more reliable for agent work, including Chrome extension connections and delegated sessions. Plugin tools now load only when needed and return less duplicate data, so more room stays available for your conversation, and a plugin error no longer disables the whole integration; newly granted write permissions also take effect in on-prem conversations you already have open. The **Kuali Build and n8n integrations move to 1.2.0**, adding Kuali app, form, and workflow editing tools plus n8n workflow and Agent tools — still governed by plugin permissions and approvals. **Outlook** inbox listings and individual message reads now include follow-up flags (keyword search can still omit them when Microsoft doesn't supply them), and a bug that stopped `/computer-use` requests from sending is fixed. Tableau remains Nightly-only. If you updated for v0.4.1, this is a routine update: Settings, About, Check for Updates.

### Around Campus

-   **[Nearly $60 million in NSF awards puts UC San Diego at the center of national AI data and compute infrastructure](https://today.ucsd.edu/photo-essays/building-the-future-of-ai-accessibility)** — Announced November 12: the National Science Foundation awarded $24.5 million to build the **National Data Platform (NDP)** and $35 million to establish the **NAIRR Operations Center**, which coordinates the Super Intelligence Infrastructure for Science. Led by SDSC Chief Data Science Officer İlkay Altıntaş, the NDP is a single environment through your browser where researchers discover, share, and connect data to computing resources — data stays on the owner's equipment and is reachable through the platform rather than copied, which matters when a single lab can hold petabytes, and compute can be drawn from a broad network of national and private resources. The operations center takes the other half: connecting NSF-funded programs, including the NDP, to the compute, tools, and expertise needed to actually run AI against that data. Dean Rajesh Gupta calls the two awards "hugely synergistic," and the access framing is explicit — the NDP was built so institutions with limited research infrastructure can reach advanced data, compute, and AI resources regardless of what they can afford locally. For campus teams, it's a reminder that the AI infrastructure work here — DSMLP, PARASOL, the shared TritonAI platform — now has a national counterpart with UC San Diego's name on it.
